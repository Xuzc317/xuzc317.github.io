Hands-on Project 2026.09 — Present
LeRobot Projects: SO-ARM101, XLeRobot & LeKiWi

My hands-on work at JoyandAI spans the LeRobot ecosystem, covering SO-ARM101, XLeRobot, and LeKiWi. This project direction brings together robot hardware setup, teleoperation, data collection, and imitation learning, from arm manipulation to mobile robot platforms.

At JoyandAI, I assembled a SO-ARM101 leader–follower pair, assigned and checked servo IDs, calibrated both arms, and brought up teleoperation. This gave me hands-on experience with the complete path from mechanical assembly and wiring to coordinated arm motion.

I collected teleoperated demonstrations using two cameras, trained an ACT imitation-learning policy on the recorded data, and ran model inference. The workflow connected robot setup, visual data collection, policy training, and inference.

I traced several hardware and calibration failures: duplicate servo IDs on parts reused from another project, reversed ID assignments on the leader arm, a cable short when the arm folded, and a midpoint offset that drove a servo beyond its physical range. JoyandAI's SO-ARM101 tutorial (v0.4.3) is a reference for the setup workflow.

XLeRobot — dual-arm mobile manipulation. The platform combines two SO-101 arms with a mobile base. Its control stack supports joint-space control, inverse-kinematics-based end-effector control, and keyboard or gamepad teleoperation of the arms and base. The documented bimanual learning workflow records multiple camera views and arm actions for ACT policy training and inference.

LeKiWi — omnidirectional mobile manipulation. A three-omniwheel base carries an SO-100/SO-101 follower arm. Leader-arm joint positions drive the arm, while keyboard commands set forward, lateral, and rotational base velocities. The LeRobot host/client workflow connects the onboard computer and operator workstation through ZeroMQ, with camera observations and robot actions recorded into demonstration episodes for imitation learning.

Current Project 2026.09 — Present
OpenMicroDuck: RL Training and Sim-to-Real

JoyandAI's OpenMicroDuck builds on the open-source MicroDuck biped with domestically sourced parts to make the platform more affordable. The robot uses domestically sourced servos and an adapted mechanical structure, requiring its control policy to be retrained for the revised hardware. The team's microduck_rl fork provides the reinforcement-learning environment.

The published training stack uses mjlab (MuJoCo Warp) and PPO, with BAM actuator modeling and domain randomization to represent hardware dynamics and their variation. Policies are exported to ONNX for real-robot inference. This provides the technical basis for adapting control policies after changes to the servos and mechanical structure.

Building on my earlier work learning the assembly workflow and testing policies in MuJoCo, I am currently training RL models and working on Sim-to-Real transfer for Walk, Stand Up, VelStand, GroundPick, Ground, and roller-standing policies. The focus is transferring policies trained for the domestically sourced servos and adapted structure to the physical robot.

Next-Phase Planning 2026.01 — Present
System Reconstruction under Distribution Shift: A Case Study in Child Speech Emotion Recognition
Intelligent Audio Research Group · Supervisor: Yan Zhou
Corresponding author: Kun Qian · Collaborator: Enze Li

Current progress: After discussing the next research directions with the subgroup lead, a PhD student, I am organizing the research plan for the next phase, building on the completed experiments and findings below.

Reproduced an existing baseline and found speaker overlap between its train and test splits. Switched to strict speaker-independent partitioning (MD5 hash, zero-leakage assertion) and re-evaluated; the earlier scores did not hold under the corrected split. Root cause identified: mean-pooled handcrafted features (162-dim) collapse the temporal axis, so the Conv1d stack slides along the feature dimension rather than time.

Rebuilt the evaluation pipeline around frame-level WavLM Base representations, 12-layer fusion, and temporal pooling, then completed a 210-run experimental suite across C-BESD, FAU Aibo, and IEMOCAP. The study tests pooling, augmentation, layer fusion, backbone fine-tuning, ablations, and cross-corpus transfer under speaker-independent splits.

On C-BESD, the three-seed weighted accuracy reached 91.87% with a frozen backbone and 96.91% after fine-tuning. Zero-shot transfer dropped sharply across corpora; target-domain fine-tuning recovered performance, with gains that depended on the target dataset. The experimental suite is complete.

Exploratory Research 2026.05 — 2026.06
SMDSL: A Domain-Specific Language for Embodied Intelligence Spatial-Motion Modeling

The idea grew out of conversations with CYDOO's CEO about vision-language-action (VLA) systems, plug-and-play industrial robotics, shared pipelines, and DSLs. I explored how a structured intermediate representation might connect high-level task intent with lower-level robot control, drawing on Fei-Fei Li's world-model research, embodied AI, A* search, image segmentation, and industrial CAD parsing.

The published SMDSL_2D prototype implements a Python-based, static 2D navigation contract with occupancy-grid geometry, distance fields, A*-derived topology, and offline validation. My longer-term research goal is to explore a Rust-based, hardware-agnostic DSL and pipeline for efficient translation from task descriptions to robot commands, beyond CUDA-centered tooling. That broader system remains an exploratory research direction.
